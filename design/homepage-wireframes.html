01 · The Drop Box — one input, zero excuses

BEST FORRestless builders and indie founders who already have a half-formed idea itching in their head and want to SEE something happen in the next 5 seconds — not read about a platform first.
WHY THEMThis audience has decision fatigue from feature-heavy AI-builder landing pages. They don't trust marketing copy; they trust the act of typing. Putting a real, working input as the literal center of the page converts them because the page IS the product demo — the first keystroke is the conversion event, not a downstream "Sign up" click. Everything else on the page is deliberately starved so the only natural action is to type.
BIG BETThe input is not a hero illustration with a CTA next to it — the input is the entire fold and it is LIVE. Typing into it immediately starts spawning visible scored opportunities below the fold (the engine reacting), so scrolling is triggered by the user's own thought, not by a scroll cue. The page proves "thought to bag" by doing it.
TRADE-OFFWorse for cautious enterprise/skeptical buyers who want proof, pricing, security, and social proof before engaging — they get almost nothing above the fold and may bounce. Also worse for SEO/skimmers: there's little crawlable benefit copy up top, and the page is useless if JS fails. It under-serves anyone who wants to comparison-shop calmly.

02 · The Loop Runs Itself: Copy-Left / Self-Playing Pipeline-Right

BEST FORTechnical-leaning solo founders and indie hackers who have been burned by "autonomous agent" demos that look magic but never ship anything real — people who buy with their eyes and distrust claims.
WHY THEMThis audience does not read paragraphs to evaluate an agent product; they watch whether it actually does the thing. The right column is a continuously self-playing simulation of the real loop (drop thought → scored opportunities → promote → build/deploy/test → slice goes live), so the core skeptic-killing differentiators (VALIDATED, actually SHIPS) are demonstrated in motion, not asserted. The left copy stays terse and meme-aware to match their irreverence and low patience.
BIG BETThe right-hand demo is not a screenshot or a looping marketing animation — it is a live, self-running pipeline that visibly executes the product's actual state machine (thought → scored bets → promote → slice build → deploy → test → SHIPPED-LIVE), with a real-looking URL/commit appearing at the end. The page sells by proving the loop completes, because "it actually ships" is the entire wedge vs Polsia/Hyperagent.
TRADE-OFFWorse for non-technical buyers, mobile-first visitors (the two-column live pipeline collapses awkwardly and loses its punch stacked vertically), and anyone who needs detailed feature/pricing breakdowns before engaging — this layout deliberately withholds a long feature list and a deep comparison grid in favor of one demonstrated narrative. It also leans hard on motion, so it underperforms for users who skim with reduced-motion or who screenshot static pages.

BEST FORThe skeptical builder-founder who already uses (or distrusts) AI coding tools — someone with enough scar tissue from "AI that almost ships" that a feature grid would bounce off them. They convert on argument, not screenshots.
WHY THEMThis audience has seen every hero+features SaaS page and has antibodies to them. They don't want a tour of buttons; they want a worldview that reframes their job ("you don't write code anymore"). A narrow, text-led scroll mimics an essay/HN-post — the native format they already trust and read to the end. By the time they hit the CTA they've internalized the thesis, so the CTA reads as "act on what you now believe," not "try a tool." Belief precedes signup for this persona.
BIG BETOne idea, defended relentlessly down a single narrow column: your role has changed from author-of-code to approver-of-shipped-bets. Every element is a paragraph in that argument; nothing is a "feature." The page's job is conversion-by-conviction, so the layout reads top-to-bottom like a manifesto with the three differentiators (VALIDATED / PORTABLE / HONEST) deployed as the proof beats — not as a feature trio in a row.
TRADE-OFFWorse for buyers who want fast at-a-glance comparison, pricing, integrations, or proof-of-product before reading — bounce-skimmers and procurement evaluators get almost nothing above the fold and no scannable feature matrix. Also worse for SEO/feature-keyword surfaces and for anyone on a tiny attention budget; this page demands a scroll commitment and punishes the impatient. It is a belief funnel, not a spec sheet.

Also worse for aspirational buyers; it's argumentative and dense, not dreamy. A founder who just wants to feel inspired bounces. It converts the convinced-of-category, unconvinced-of-vendor crowd at the cost of everyone earlier in the funnel.

10 · The Board Is The Pitch

BEST FORShow-me-the-product operators — founders/indie hackers who've been burned by demo-ware and AI-agent vaporware. They scroll past copy and instinctively look for the actual screen. They want to see the Companies board, the scores, the slice-by-slice build state, and a deploy URL before they read a single benefit bullet.
WHY THEMThis audience converts on evidence, not adjectives. Competitors (Polsia/Hyperagent) sell with abstract "autonomous agent" promises; this layout instead leads with a giant, legible wireframe of the real Companies board where they can literally read a thought turning into a scored opportunity turning into a shipping company with a live URL. The product's three differentiators (VALIDATED scores, PORTABLE git repo, RELIABLE human-approved slices) are all things you can SEE as UI affordances — so the screenshot itself IS the argument. Putting the artifact first earns the right to their attention faster than any headline could.
BIG BETThe hero is not a headline with a screenshot beside it — the hero IS the screenshot. A near-full-bleed, annotated wireframe of the live Companies board dominates the fold; copy is reduced to a thin overlay strip and call-out labels pinned to real UI regions. The whole page is structured as a guided tour of one screen, not a list of features.
TRADE-OFFWorse for skim-readers who want a one-line value prop and an emotional brand hook, and worse for buyers who don't yet know what an "opportunity score" or "slice" is — the screenshot-first approach assumes enough product literacy to read a board. It's also heavier (large hero asset, annotation layer) so it's slower on mobile and demands a real responsive fallback. A benefit-led or story-led archetype would onboard a cold, non-technical visitor faster.
